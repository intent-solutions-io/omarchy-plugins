# Audience and attention-quality hypothesis test

> This record tests an owner hypothesis and translates reference qualities into original product experiments. It does not authorize copied branding, a target-age decision, a product claim, minor recruitment, commerce, or implementation.

## Question

Is Roblox's audience aging upward enough to justify an older-skewing presentation for the Omarchy kids typing adventure, and can the attention and purchase qualities associated with leading challenge entertainment and creator worlds be tested without copying them or adopting manipulative product mechanics?

## Verdict

**Partly verified.** Roblox has a large and growing older audience, and the majority of its currently age-checked daily active users are at least 13. Roblox is not, however, majority adult. Its own current data says 73 percent of the age-checked cohort is under 18. The evidence supports an age-flexible, non-childish direction and separate cohort testing. It does not select an omaType audience, prove that Roblox users need typing instruction, or prove that their spending behavior transfers to a parent-purchased educational desktop app.

### Current figures

The most current located distribution is the Roblox Q2 2026 shareholder letter. It reports 35 percent under 13, 38 percent ages 13 to 17, and 27 percent over 18 among users who had completed an age check. It also reports that U.S. over-18 daily active users grew 32 percent year over year, U.S. over-18 hours grew 27 percent, and the U.S. 18 to 34 cohort led those measures at 42 percent and 37 percent growth. Roblox states that U.S. over-18 users monetize more than 50 percent higher than under-18 users. Global age-check penetration was 57 percent, so the distribution did not cover all daily active users.

Three consecutive company snapshots are directionally consistent rather than independent confirmations. Q4 2025 reported 35 percent under 13, 38 percent ages 13 to 17, and 27 percent over 18 at 45 percent global age-check penetration. Q1 2026 reported 36 percent, 38 percent, and 26 percent at 51 percent penetration. Q2 2026 returned to 35 percent, 38 percent, and 27 percent at 57 percent penetration. This supports a stable current picture among age-checked users, not a clean longitudinal proof that the whole population is growing older.

The 2023 Roblox newsroom statement reported that more than 55 percent of users were over 13, ages 17 to 24 represented 22 percent of the community, and that band was the fastest-growing demographic at the time. That older statement used self-reported age. Roblox's 2025 Form 10-K says the new age-checked series is not comparable with the historical self-reported series.

### Measurement limitations

- Roblox is the source of every platform-specific figure and has an investor and strategy interest in presenting growth and monetization favorably.
- A daily active user is an active registered account, not necessarily one unique person.
- Age checking was incomplete, and chat access creates selection effects. Roblox's Form 10-K says the age-checked population may not represent the entire user base.
- Current age checks combine estimation, identity verification, parent-provided data, and other signals. Their accuracy and adoption can change.
- Global distribution, U.S. growth, and U.S. monetization are different denominators and must not be blended.
- Roblox is a broad social and creation platform. Its audience cannot validate the market for a local-first typing game.
- Roblox's Q2 2026 filing reports about 1.6 million average daily unique payers against 123 million daily active users. The measures are account based and not identical populations, but the small ratio is another reason not to equate attention with purchase intent.

## Product inference

The correct inference is **mature enough to respect older children, flexible enough not to exclude younger learners**. The previous 8 to 12 assumption was too broad for one undifferentiated presentation and should not silently become 13 to 17 merely because Roblox is aging.

Discovery should compare at least two separately reported candidate cohorts, initially ages 9 to 11 and 12 to 14, subject to qualified learning, safeguarding, accessibility, and privacy review. The product should use the same learning truth while varying reading load, character presentation, humor, challenge framing, and visual intensity. A child can quick-start without disclosing an exact birth date. A parent may choose a coarse local band only when the curriculum rationale requires it.

No combined result may hide a poor outcome in either cohort. The owner selects the primary market only after cohort evidence, parent demand, support capacity, and curriculum fit are visible.

## Transferable qualities, not copied brands

| Quality | Original prototype treatment | What is prohibited | Evidence signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instant challenge clarity | Show one concrete world problem and one meaningful action within the opening seconds. | copied creator identity, thumbnail grammar, slogan, voice, or likeness | child explains the goal and available choice before facilitator help |
| Escalating surprise | Each accurate sequence changes the route, machine, weather, or world state and culminates in a bounded set-piece. | false urgency, endless escalation, flashing overload, or spending pressure | comprehension stays stable while replay interest rises |
| Inhabitable world | Restore and personalize an original local world that visibly remembers mastery. | copied Roblox presentation, public child identity, open chat, public UGC, or virtual currency | child explores or revisits voluntarily after the required task ends |
| Creator agency | Let the learner combine approved local parts to solve or decorate after mastery. | arbitrary scripts, external asset fetches, publishing, popularity metrics, or pay-to-create basics | child produces meaningfully different valid outcomes |
| Adaptive intelligence | Use transparent deterministic coaching first, with human-reviewed AI assistance only in the authoring workflow. | pretending rules are generative AI, uploading child input, hidden model calls, or open child chatbot | coaching is specific, understood, and improves the next independent attempt |
| Omarchy identity | Keyboard-first control, precise system feedback, theme awareness, local-first trust, and a later shortcut workshop. | an adult terminal skin pasted over a generic worksheet | child perceives a coherent game and Omarchy users recognize native craft |

## Falsifiable discovery design

### H1: presentation maturity

Blind-test two original art and tone directions with the 9 to 11 and 12 to 14 candidate cohorts. Do not name reference brands. Measure comprehension, self-description such as "for kids like me," visual preference, embarrassment or babyishness signals, accessibility, and desire to continue. Reject any direction that wins aggregate preference by alienating one intended cohort.

### H2: game attention

Compare three 3 to 5 minute typing-driven greyboxes with a minimally dressed drill and an attractive non-typing alternative. Randomize presentation order where the approved protocol permits. Measure goal comprehension, meaningful choices, calm accuracy, assistance, frustration, completion, immediate next-choice behavior, and unprompted replay within the approved follow-up window. Effects alone do not count as gameplay.

### H3: ethical return motivation

After the required task, offer a real stop, replay, and alternate activity with equal visual prominence. A voluntary return must occur without a streak, countdown, prize lottery, facilitator prompt, public status, or threatened loss. Record why the child chose to return. Failure means redesigning the loop, not increasing pressure.

### H4: parent willingness to pay

Only after a usable free proof exists, show parents the permanent free boundary and concrete optional additions. Test comprehension, perceived fairness, one-time price ranges, support expectations, and a separate double-opt-in interest action. Stated enthusiasm is weak evidence. A later owner-approved parent-only purchase experiment is stronger evidence, but no child data, child-facing prompt, live checkout, or paid build is authorized by this record.

## Stop conditions

- The child cannot explain what typed input changed.
- Accuracy falls because spectacle obscures the prompt or rewards mashing.
- The experience needs copied brand recognition to be understood.
- The older treatment makes younger intended learners feel excluded, or the younger treatment feels babyish to the older cohort.
- Replay depends on urgency, random rewards, public comparison, repeated prompting, or loss aversion.
- Parent interest depends on withholding core correction, accessibility, deletion, or the complete free alphabet path.
- "AI" is required as a label but adds no observable value, or requires child data to leave the device.

## Decision impact

1. Keep the audience open as a hypothesis and test ages 9 to 11 and 12 to 14 separately before selecting the primary cohort.
2. Make both prototype art directions non-childish and original; vary maturity without gendered or age-stereotyped rewards.
3. Test attention through comprehension, meaningful choice, voluntary replay, and calm accuracy, not session length alone.
4. Treat parent willingness to pay as a separate adult-side hypothesis after free gameplay proof.
5. Keep online multiplayer, public UGC, chat, runtime generative AI, and child-facing monetization outside the current authorization.
