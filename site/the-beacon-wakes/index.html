Light the beacon together.

You do not need to know how to teach typing. Your job is to make the first few minutes calm, let the child make meaningful choices, and protect their ability to stop.

  1. Invite, do not assign.Try: “I found a game where your typing wakes up a broken world. Want to try the first mission with me?”
  2. Give real choices.Let the child choose whether to try, who touches the keyboard first, and whether you stay nearby.
  3. Model one mistake.Type one short message yourself. Make a normal mistake and calmly correct it so errors do not feel like failure.
  4. Hand over control.Let the child drive the mission. Notice strategy, correction, and persistence before speed.
  5. End while it is still positive.Stop at a natural ending or whenever the child asks. There is no streak to protect and no penalty for stopping.

One useful question after play

What part felt interesting, confusing, or annoying?
Listen to the answer. Do not demand another round to improve a score.

If they say no, the answer is no.

You can ask once what made the idea unappealing and try another day. Do not threaten to remove another activity, compare siblings, hide a lesson inside play, or use a reward as pressure to continue through distress.

Adjust the environment first.

The browser demo is untimed and has sound and reduced-motion controls. It does not include text-size controls yet, so stop if the experience is hard to read or hands begin to hurt.
