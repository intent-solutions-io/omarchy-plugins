Start here

The basic idea, what would arrive, and what you would do.

Plain answerWhat exactly is BLUE GOLD BLUE?

It is a product journey we are researching for people who want to move from Windows to Omarchy without becoming backup or Linux experts. BLUE is a physical personal cloud that saves and verifies supported files. GOLD is the installation key. Then BLUE brings the supported files back. It is not a finished product yet.

Plain answerWhat is Omarchy?

Omarchy is the Linux-based computer environment being evaluated as the first destination for this project. On the same-computer journey, it would become your main environment in place of Windows. Some familiar Windows programs may not run there, so application compatibility has to be checked before any switch.

Leading ideaWhat would arrive at my door?

The leading idea is a prepared BLUE storage device and a separate GOLD installation key, with very simple instructions. BLUE would be yours to keep. The exact devices, packaging, contents, shipping method, and whether every offer includes both devices are not final.

Leading ideaDo I send you my computer?

Not for the basic kit being considered. The intended work happens on your computer with the devices in your hands. Assisted service and preconfigured-computer options are separate ideas, and their shipping or handling process has not been defined.

Three stepsWhat would I actually have to do?

A future released process would start by plugging BLUE into Windows and choosing to save your stuff. GOLD could proceed only after BLUE reports READY TO SWITCH and GOLD checks that this is the right computer, the right BLUE, and an untampered installation release. No customer should connect installation media based on this research page.

Safety ruleWhat happens to Windows when I plug in BLUE?

BLUE is being designed to read from the Windows computer and copy supported files onto BLUE. It must not delete, rename, move, or format files on the Windows computer. A prepared BLUE may open a guided application, but the exact Windows prompts are still being tested. If a future bring-your-own-drive option needs to prepare that separate drive, it must explain the change and ask first.

Plain answerWhat exactly is GOLD?

GOLD is not a password or a license code. It is the planned physical installation key. Before installing anything, a released GOLD must confirm READY TO SWITCH, the right computer and BLUE, an untampered release, the intended destination disk, and your deliberate approval. No customer-ready GOLD exists today.

Safety ruleWhat happens if I plug in GOLD before BLUE is ready?

A released GOLD must refuse to change the computer unless the same migration has a fresh, valid READY TO SWITCH result from BLUE. Missing, stale, altered, wrong-computer, or wrong-BLUE evidence must stop the process. Do not test this today. No customer-ready GOLD media or procedure exists.

Third stepWhat happens when I plug BLUE in after Omarchy starts?

You would insert the same BLUE used for this migration and choose BRING MY STUFF HOME. A released process should recognize the migration, keep the verified copy on BLUE unchanged, restore supported data without running copied programs, and show what returned or still needs attention. This restore flow is not proven today.

UndecidedWhich port, cable, adapter, or power setup will I need?

The final BLUE and GOLD connections are not selected. A finished offer must state whether it uses USB-A, USB-C, an included cable, or an approved adapter before you buy. The instructions should also say when to connect power, prevent sleep, remove BLUE, and insert GOLD. Hubs and unapproved adapters may be excluded until tested.

Safety ruleHow will I tell BLUE and GOLD apart and know they are genuine?

A future release must make the devices visually distinct and cryptographically authenticate the exact approved installer and media. GOLD must reject an altered, stale, withdrawn, substituted, or untrusted release. Use only the device assigned to your migration through an approved channel. None of those controls or the replacement process is proven today.

No. Do not build or connect BLUE or GOLD media from this page. No customer kit, installation runtime, or released safety procedure exists today. Wait for an explicitly released, supported product. This page grants no installation permission.

Current factIs Omarchy involved in this project?

Not supportedCould I use BLUE GOLD BLUE to move to Ubuntu, Fedora, Mint, or another Linux system?

Your computer and the switch

Which machines are being considered and what happens to Windows.

Research targetWill this work on my computer?

The initial research target is one primary user on a personal Windows 10 or 11 computer with explicitly supported Intel or AMD 64-bit hardware and a straightforward internal drive. A final compatibility list does not exist. BLUE must check the actual computer and stop when required evidence is missing or unsupported.

Future ideaCan I check my computer before I buy or plug anything in?

Not today. There is no released checker or final supported-computer list. A future non-destructive check should review your exact computer, Windows version, storage, security settings, important apps, and devices, then show what is supported, unknown, or blocked before you buy or change anything.

UndecidedCan I try Omarchy before Windows is erased?

No customer preview path is promised yet. A temporary boot-time check may be needed to prove hardware compatibility, but that is not the same as a safe trial or dual-boot setup. Do not download or boot installation media from this page. Any future preview must explain its limits and leave Windows unchanged.

Not in V1What about Macs, Linux computers, older Windows versions, or ARM PCs?

They are not part of the first product boundary. The design may support more source computers later, but the first proof is intentionally narrow: supported Windows 10 or 11 computers using Intel or AMD 64-bit processors.

Known limitWhat about a work computer, shared family computer, or several Windows users?

The first target is a personal, non-company-managed computer with one primary user. Work-managed devices, shared systems, and multiple user profiles are outside the initial proof because they add permissions, ownership, policy, and privacy questions. Do not use the kit on an employer's or shared computer unless a later, separately tested process explicitly supports it.

Still provingWill I need administrator access, and what about BitLocker or Secure Boot?

The current standard-user proof cannot read every BitLocker or Secure Boot setting, and no customer elevation path is released. A future client should use the least access it needs and explain any approved administrator prompt before it appears. If a required security or firmware state cannot be checked safely, BLUE must say REVIEW or STOP.

Still provingDo I need to turn off Windows security or give anyone my administrator password?

No unreleased process should ask you to disable Defender or antivirus, bypass Windows security, or send anyone a password. A future client may need an approved administrator prompt for specific checks, but that behavior is not proven. An undocumented exception must produce REVIEW or STOP, not instructions to weaken your computer.

Known limitWhat if I have several internal drives, partitions, external drives, or network storage?

The first proof is limited to one straightforward internal Windows drive. Extra internal drives, partitions, attached drives, network shares, and home storage are outside that proof. Their presence must prevent READY TO SWITCH unless a separately tested policy handles them and you review every exclusion.

Two journeysDo I switch this computer or move to a new one?

Both journeys are being considered. The same-computer path would replace Windows after BLUE passes every safety gate. The new-computer path would save from the old computer and restore onto a separately qualified computer, leaving the old machine intact as a fallback. The new-computer option is a future product path, not a current offer.

ImportantWill GOLD remove Windows from my computer?

On the same-computer journey, yes. The intended GOLD installation can replace Windows and erase the existing system disk. That is why GOLD must remain locked out until BLUE has independently verified the required files and the computer has passed compatibility checks.

Not promisedWill this create a Windows and Omarchy dual-boot computer?

Do not expect dual boot. The simple same-computer journey is being designed as a controlled replacement, not a menu that keeps two operating systems side by side. A new-computer journey may reduce the risk of changing the original Windows computer, but it is a future path and has not been tested.

Known limitCan BLUE put Windows back if I change my mind?

That is not currently promised. BLUE is being designed to protect and restore supported personal data, not to create a complete Windows system image with every program, license, and setting. Reinstalling Windows would be a separate recovery process.

Design targetCan I stop after BLUE and keep using Windows?

A future released BLUE step should leave Windows and the source files unchanged, so stopping before GOLD should leave Windows usable and does not commit you to a switch. That behavior is not proven in a customer runtime today. Never use GOLD after REVIEW, STOP, or an incomplete or uncertain BLUE step.

Not promisedDoes BLUE preserve my Windows license or activation?

No Windows license, product key, or activation transfer is promised. BLUE is intended to protect selected personal data, not a Windows installation. Reinstalling or reactivating Windows would be a separate recovery process.

Out of scopeCan BLUE rescue a computer that is already failing?

Do not rely on this research project as a rescue service. A failing drive or unstable computer needs a separate recovery approach, and continued use can make data loss worse. The first product scope is a controlled migration from a working, supported computer.

Your files, apps, and accounts

What should move automatically, what needs another app, and what may need attention.

PlannedIs BLUE a complete backup, and what will it save?

No. BLUE is not intended to clone the entire Windows system. The first version is being tested for supported personal data such as Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Pictures, nested folders, and selected browser or familiarity information. It will not recreate Windows, installed programs, every setting, licenses, passwords, or signed-in sessions. A future released process must show an itemized record of what was found, copied, skipped, or needs attention before GOLD.

Known limitWhat about OneDrive, Dropbox, or files that are only online?

If a file is only online, BLUE cannot protect it until the actual file is local and verified. A name or shortcut shown in Windows is not enough. No live OneDrive, Dropbox, or other provider-acquisition path is released or proven. BLUE must report the placeholder as unprotected and stop if the file is required; do not sign in or assume BLUE can download it.

UndecidedCan I choose what BLUE does or does not copy?

The final selection experience is not set. A customer should be able to understand and deliberately exclude personal content, but an excluded required item cannot still count as protected. BLUE must show what will be included, what was left out, and whether those choices prevent READY TO SWITCH.

Review firstCan I review my files, apps, and devices before GOLD?

A released process should show what BLUE found, what it protected, what it skipped, and which apps or devices need attention. You should be able to mark the things you cannot live without as critical. An unknown or unsupported critical item must produce REVIEW or STOP before GOLD. This customer review is not proven yet.

Known limitWill my Windows programs move to Omarchy?

Not automatically. BLUE may identify programs and classify the likely path as a native Omarchy app, a web app, an alternative, or unsupported. It will not copy Windows program files and pretend they will work. If an essential program has no acceptable path, the result should tell you to stop before GOLD.

Check firstWhat about Microsoft Office, Adobe apps, QuickBooks, games, or other must-have software?

Each important program needs a truthful compatibility answer before you switch. Some may have web versions, Linux versions, or acceptable alternatives. Others may not work or may need a new license or sign-in. BLUE should ask what is essential and produce REVIEW or STOP when the answer is uncertain or unacceptable.

Check firstWill my photos and documents still open?

Restoring a file and being able to use it are related but different questions. Common formats may open in native or alternative apps, but unusual, proprietary, damaged, or application-specific files may not. Compatibility testing must cover both the file copy and the destination app needed to use it.

Known limitWill passwords and signed-in sessions move?

Do not assume they will. Saved passwords, browser sessions, passkeys, license activations, and app credentials have different security rules. The conservative expectation is that you will sign in again. BLUE will not expose or copy secrets merely to make the move look easier.

PlannedWhat about browser bookmarks, email, contacts, and calendars?

Bookmarks and other safe familiarity data may be translated when a supported path is proven. Email, contacts, calendars, and locally stored archives are not currently promised and require a separately proven path. Expect to sign in again. Your Windows account, every preference, account secrets, and live sessions will not simply become an Omarchy account.

Design goalWhere will my files go, and will the new computer feel familiar?

Supported personal folders should return to predictable places, and selected familiarity items such as wallpaper, bookmarks, fonts, and safe settings may be translated. The goal is to reduce the shock of switching, not to make Omarchy pretend to be Windows. A restore report must show where things went and what still needs attention.

Report clearlyWhat if a file is locked, encrypted, damaged, hidden, or in an unusual place?

BLUE should never silently skip it and still say everything is safe. Supported exceptions must be listed with a plain-language reason. Important unreadable or unavailable content must produce REVIEW or STOP until it is recovered, deliberately excluded, or handled through an approved process.

Check firstWill my Wi-Fi, sound, screen, keyboard, printer, and other devices work?

There is no universal guarantee. Compatibility must be checked for your exact computer and Omarchy release, including Wi-Fi, Ethernet, graphics, displays, audio, keyboard, mouse or trackpad, webcam, Bluetooth, printers, and anything else you depend on. An unknown or unsupported critical device must produce REVIEW or STOP before GOLD.

Safety ruleWhat if restore finds an existing file, stops halfway, or needs to be retried?

Restore is being designed to avoid silently overwriting an important file, reject broken or unsafe restore instructions, record every conflict, and support a safe retry. An interrupted restore must not damage the verified copy on BLUE, place files outside the intended restore area, run copied programs, or claim that everything returned. The exact recovery choices still need controlled testing.

Report clearlyHow will I know what was restored and what still needs attention?

A future released restore must end with an itemized report showing what returned, what was skipped or conflicted, what needs another app, and which accounts need you to sign in again. Missing or uncertain items must stay visible. A progress bar or copied-file count alone is not proof that the restore is complete.

Safety and privacy

How the design is supposed to fail safely when something is wrong or unknown.

Safety ruleWhat does “safe” mean?

READY TO SWITCH must mean more than a percentage, hash, signature, or completed progress bar. A future released process must account for the required inventory, independently verify the obtained file contents, check source health and capacity, use current compatibility evidence, and bind the result to this migration, computer, BLUE, and exact destination release. Missing, stale, conflicting, or unknown required evidence must produce STOP or REVIEW.

Safety ruleHow will GOLD avoid erasing the wrong drive?

Only the explicitly approved system drive should ever be eligible. The design requires positive computer and drive identification, protection of BLUE and every other removable device, and a final authorization check. If the intended drive cannot be identified with certainty, GOLD must stop. Those controls are not yet proven on real customer hardware, so GOLD is not ready for customer use.

Still provingWhat if the copy fails, power goes out, or I unplug BLUE?

The intended behavior is to stop without declaring success, keep an understandable record, and support a safe retry. Exact interruption, resume, and recovery behavior still needs fault-injection testing. You should never be told to continue to GOLD after an incomplete or uncertain protection step.

Still provingCan I keep using my computer while BLUE saves, and when can I unplug it?

The exact rule is not set. Files can change while you work, so a released process must explain what you can keep using, when apps should close, when the computer must stay awake or plugged into power, and when BLUE is safe to remove. It must recheck required data before READY TO SWITCH. Do not unplug BLUE during an active or uncertain operation.

Strong adviceShould I keep another backup before switching?

Yes. Do not treat one local BLUE copy as disaster protection. A future customer release must define an independent backup and recovery requirement before GOLD. Until that requirement and recovery path are tested and published, do not proceed with a destructive migration.

Safety ruleCan I mix BLUE or GOLD devices from different computers or migrations?

No. The intended design binds READY TO SWITCH to one migration and the approved computer. GOLD must reject a missing, stale, altered, wrong-computer, or wrong-BLUE record. Do not borrow or swap devices between migrations, even if they look identical. This binding is a required safety control and is still being implemented and tested.

Still provingWhat if GOLD loses power, fails, or the computer will not start afterward?

An interrupted installation can leave the computer without a working operating system after Windows has been removed. There is no proven rollback that recreates the original Windows installation, programs, licenses, settings, or startup state. BLUE is intended to preserve the verified supported files, but it is not a complete system image and does not repair the computer. Recovery and retry rules must be proven on disposable hardware before GOLD is ready.

Still provingCan I stop after GOLD starts?

No safe stopping procedure is proven yet. Do not start GOLD until a released process tells you exactly when and how stopping is safe. Once installation begins, never improvise by unplugging a device or shutting down the computer; follow only the released recovery instructions.

UndecidedWhat if BLUE is lost, stolen, or damaged?

A local device can be lost or fail, so BLUE must not be described as permanent or disaster-proof storage. Encryption, recovery copies, replacement, and failure handling are still being proven. Losing BLUE after Windows has been replaced could be serious, which is why the commercial design cannot move ahead without a tested recovery story.

PlannedWill BLUE be encrypted, and what happens if I lose the recovery key?

The provisional design encrypts file contents, filenames, catalogs, and reports while keeping private recovery material separate from BLUE. Intent Solutions would not keep an escrow copy or backdoor. Some basic media facts, such as approximate sizes or counts, may remain visible. If every separate recovery package is lost, the data may be permanently unrecoverable; if one is stolen, that migration may be exposed. This is not a production guarantee yet.

Design targetCan I recover my files if the BLUE software or Intent Solutions is unavailable?

The design aims to keep your data recoverable through a documented, non-proprietary path where practical. The final storage format, authorized decryption tool, recovery package, and customer recovery steps are not proven. Do not assume independent recovery is available today, and never treat one BLUE device as your only backup.

Design targetIs BLUE an internet cloud, and are my files uploaded?

BLUE is physical local storage that stays with you, not an internet cloud, with file contents kept local by default. Any optional network step must disclose exactly what leaves the computer and must not send file contents, credentials, keys, or browser secrets by default.

Privacy targetCan Intent Solutions or support see my files?

The migration keeps file contents on your computer and BLUE. Support must use redacted reports and must not ask for personal files, passwords, recovery keys, or unrestricted computer logs. Any optional remote access would require separate, explicit consent and published limits.

Still provingCould BLUE copy malware or dangerous files?

Migration must treat saved content as untrusted. The restore design must not automatically run migrated programs, scripts, or unknown executables. Malware scanning and customer guidance still need a defined, tested policy, so BLUE cannot promise that every saved file is harmless.

Safety ruleWhat if BLUE does not have enough room?

BLUE must measure supported data and usable capacity before it claims protection. If there is not enough room, it should stop and explain what is needed. It must not quietly leave required files behind or approve GOLD based on a partial copy.

Safety ruleWhat happens if BLUE says REVIEW or STOP?

Do not use GOLD. Neither REVIEW nor STOP authorizes installation. Do not alter Windows or overwrite the last verified BLUE copy. BLUE may contain partial or failed work, so follow only the released recovery procedure and never count it as protected until it is verified again. If no procedure is provided, stop and wait for help. This behavior is not proven yet.

No terms yetWhat happens if I lose access to the computer or my data?

No recovery guarantee, response-time commitment, warranty, refund, replacement, or data-recovery service is available today. Those terms and a tested recovery process must be published before sale. Do not use an unreleased process, and do not rely on one local BLUE device as your only backup.
